### What changed since 2026-09-02

The project owner re-supplied the spreadsheet on 2026-09-05 with four new
columns: `availability_status` and three PII columns (`Name`, `Phone Number`,
`Voter ID`, none of which is imported). A fifth existing column, `Society Type`,
was adopted into the schema as `society_type` on the same date.

**The STOP condition is no longer met.** On 2026-09-02 the box was ticked at
PROCEED WITH SPEC AMENDMENT despite the literal STOP condition (no availability
marker) being true, on the owner's decision. That marker now exists, so the
third box is false on its own terms rather than by exception. The second box is
still the right one, because seven §3.1 fields remain unpublished.

**One caveat the marker does not remove.** Per `data/SOURCE_NOTES.md`, the
`availability_status` values are randomly generated — an independent coin flip
per row — as are rent, deposit and the other descriptive columns. The
*mechanism* is now real and testable end to end (curation drops rows marked
unavailable; the overlay and the booking re-check own everything after that);
the *values* are synthetic. Availability in this bundle is not a fact about any
real property.

### What this dataset is, in one paragraph

2,370 listings over 464 localities: 128 localities sit at the 10-per-locality
ceiling and 336 below it (minimum 1). The drop from 3,216 over 566 is not a
data loss — it is the new marker doing its job: 4,648 of the 9,180 rows are
marked unavailable and are dropped at curation, which also empties 102
localities entirely. Every record carries coordinates, rent, deposit, BHK,
bedrooms, bathrooms, balconies, property type, furnishing, floor area, a yes/no
parking flag, a builder name, a gated/non-gated flag and an availability flag;
total floors is stated for apartments only. Dedupe merged 43 records into 42
winners. Per `data/SOURCE_NOTES.md`, coordinates and locality names are real but
rent, deposit, availability and every other descriptive column are randomly
generated placeholders — the grounding discipline is testable on them; the
prices are not market facts and the demo must not present them as such.

### Two costs the plan did not anticipate (for the owner, before Phase 1)

1. **464 localities.** Task 1.1 asks for 1–3 hand-picked guide pages per
   locality; Task 1.3 runs 8 OpenStreetMap queries per listing (2,370 × 8 =
   18,960 calls). Both were sized for a source with tens of localities, not
   hundreds. This is smaller than the 25,728 calls the 2026-09-02 bundle implied,
   but the same order of magnitude. The reverse-geocoded locality names also
   include building and estate names (Ajmera Infinity Apartments, Concorde
   Manhattans Apartments, AB Gardenia and others) alongside true neighbourhoods,
   so some of the 464 are not localities in the spec's sense and would have no
   guide document to cite. Restricting the bundle to a named locality set, or to
   localities with at least N rows, is a spec §1 decision the owner can still
   take before Task 1.1; the curation code needs no change to honour it.
2. **The marker's values are synthetic.** The stale-listing behaviour (spec
   §3.1, §6.43) can now be demonstrated from the data as well as through the
   operator toggle, but what it demonstrates is a coin flip, not a real
   delisting.

Cost of the import: 9,180 rows, 6 seconds end to end — measured 2026-09-05: import 2.6 s, dedupe and curation 2.6 s, manifest 0.7 s.
